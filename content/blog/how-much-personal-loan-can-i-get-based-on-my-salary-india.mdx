## Introduction

If you earn ₹20,000 a month, you may wonder whether a personal loan is even possible. If your salary is ₹50,000 or ₹80,000, you probably want to know the realistic maximum you can borrow without straining your budget.

Salary is one of the biggest factors banks and NBFCs consider when deciding your personal loan eligibility. It helps lenders assess how much EMI you can comfortably repay every month.

The actual amount you get approved for also depends on your existing EMIs, credit score, and job stability. Most lenders follow similar income and EMI rules, but approval criteria can vary slightly.

To understand the real impact on your monthly budget, use an [EMI calculator](/calculators/personal-loan-emi-calculator) to test different loan amounts and tenures.

## Quick Answer

### Estimated Loan Eligibility by Salary

Here are realistic approximate ranges based on current lending practices in India, assuming a good credit score, stable job, and no major existing EMIs. Actual offers vary by lender.

- ₹20,000 salary → ₹1.5 lakh – ₹3 lakh
- ₹30,000 salary → ₹2.5 lakh – ₹5 lakh
- ₹50,000 salary → ₹4 lakh – ₹8–10 lakh
- ₹80,000+ salary → ₹8 lakh – ₹15–20 lakh+

These are broad estimates. Lenders often use a combination of income multiplier and EMI-to-income limits.

## How Banks Decide Your Loan Amount

Banks mainly use two methods:

- **Income multiplier method:** They multiply your net monthly income by a factor, usually around 10 to 24 times. A higher salary or stronger profile can attract a higher multiplier.
- **EMI-to-income ratio (FOIR/DTI):** Lenders prefer that your total EMIs, including the new personal loan EMI, do not exceed 40–50% of your net take-home salary. Many aim for a safer 30–40%.

They also consider your existing loans, repayment tenure, and overall income stability.

## Salary-Based Breakdown

### Salary ₹20,000 – ₹30,000

At this level, many lenders set a minimum income threshold, often around ₹15,000–₹25,000 depending on city and lender.

- Expected loan range: ₹1.5 lakh to ₹4–5 lakh
- EMI limit: Keep total EMIs under ₹8,000–₹12,000 per month
- Typical options: Smaller NBFCs or digital lenders may be more flexible than traditional banks

### Salary ₹30,000 – ₹50,000

You usually get better eligibility and more lender choices.

- Expected loan range: ₹3 lakh to ₹8 lakh
- EMI limit: Roughly ₹12,000–₹20,000 per month
- More banks and NBFCs become accessible if your profile is clean

### Salary ₹50,000 – ₹1,00,000+

Higher salary opens larger loan amounts and potentially lower interest rates.

- Expected loan range: ₹5 lakh to ₹15–20 lakh or more
- EMI limit: ₹20,000–₹40,000+ per month, depending on exact income and existing obligations
- You may qualify for premium offers if you have a strong credit history

## Factors That Affect Loan Amount

Several things beyond salary influence the final approved amount:

- **Credit score (CIBIL):** Scores above 750 usually help you get higher limits and better rates
- **Existing loans and EMIs:** High current debt lowers the room for a new EMI
- **Job stability:** Longer tenure with the current employer improves approval chances
- **Employer type:** Government, PSU, or reputed MNC employment is often viewed more favourably
- **Other factors:** Age, city of residence, and income proof strength also play a role

## FAQ

### Can I get a personal loan with ₹25,000 salary?

Yes, many lenders accept applications at this level, though the approved amount may be modest depending on your credit profile and existing EMIs.

### What is the minimum salary for a personal loan in India?

It usually ranges from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month for salaried applicants, varying by lender and city.

### How much EMI is safe for my salary?

A safe guideline is to keep total EMIs under 40% of your net monthly income. Staying closer to 30–35% is even better for financial comfort.

### Can I increase my loan amount after initial approval?

It is difficult. It is better to apply for the right amount upfront based on your eligibility and repayment capacity.

### Does a higher salary guarantee a higher loan amount?

Not always. Credit score, existing debts, and job stability also matter significantly.
